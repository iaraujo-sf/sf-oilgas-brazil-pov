# Brazil Downstream Oil & Gas Market Overview

## Market Structure

Brazil's downstream fuel distribution market is one of the most complex operating environments in the energy industry. The market comprises 155+ active distributors, approximately 44,000 gas stations (51% branded, 49% white-flag/unbranded), 500 licensed TRR resellers, and 299 distribution terminals.

## Value Chain: Production to End User

| Stage | Players | Key Activities |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Production/Import | 17 Refineries (68%), 361 Ethanol Plants (20%), 58 Biodiesel Plants (5%), Importers (7%) | Refining, blending, importing |
| Distribution | 155 Distributors | Storage, transportation, biofuel blending, additives |
| Retail | ~44K Stations (51% branded, 49% white-flag) | Urban/Highway fuel retail, convenience |
| TRR | 500 licensed resellers | Diesel, Gasoline and Ethanol to end users |
| End Users | Small/Medium + Large businesses | Hospitals, malls, logistics, mining, agribusiness, thermal plants |

## Market Size & Growth

- **Annual Consumption:** 133M cubic meters per year
- **CAGR 2010-2024:** 2.5% (G+E+D volume)
- **Import Market:** US$51 billion (largest in South America)
- **Imported Volume:** ~12% of total consumption
- **Biofuel CAGR:** 3.8% (2010-2024)
- **Top 3 Market Share:** 58% (Vibra, Raízen, Ipiranga)
- **Total Market Revenue:** $76B+ (distribution + retail)
- **Annual Volume:** ~140 billion liters (all fuel types)
- **Loyalty Members:** 40M+ across top programs
- **Road-Based Freight:** 65% drives diesel demand

## Key Market Characteristics

- Fuel mix dominated by diesel and gasoline
- 1 gas station per 1,000 vehicles on average
- Consistently outpaced GDP growth for decades
- 3 distributors representing 58% of the market
- Strong biofuels component with 3.8% CAGR

## Transportation Trends as Key Driver

- 65% of freight transported by road (highway dependence)
- Heavy investment in toll roads vs. railways
- Continental distances make fuel logistics critical
- Total Fleet CAGR: 1.3% (2014-2024)
- Light Vehicles CAGR: 1.2% (2014-2024)
- Vehicle penetration remains below developed peers — room for growth

### Limited Electrification Impact

- Brazil has competitive advantages in biofuels
- Heavy and light EV prices remain high + overtaxed with state reform
- National charging network underinvested
- Heavy vehicles: electrification only viable for last mile
- The current transportation environment will not change in the near future — fuel demand remains structurally supported

## Biofuels & Ethanol Market

Brazil is the world's second-largest biofuels market, supported by a steadily expanding flex-fuel fleet and rising sales volumes.

- **Global Position:** 2nd largest biofuels market
- **Biofuel Sales CAGR:** 4.0% (2017-2024)
- **Flex-Fuel Fleet:** 80% of light vehicles (2023)
- **Global Ethanol Share:** 26.7% (2030 estimate)

### Mandates & Policy

- **Ethanol mandate:** 27% in gasoline (range 22-35%)
- **Biodiesel mandate:** 15% in diesel (up to 20% by 2030)
- "Fuel of the Future" Law (2024) increased blend levels
- Expected to increase biofuels demand by 3+ billion liters

### RenovaBio — Carbon Credits

Producers issue CBIOs (carbon credits) traded on stock exchange. Distributors required to purchase CBIOs to offset CO2 emissions. Volume defined by ANP based on previous year's distribution. CBIO compliance tracking and optimization is a natural Salesforce + Data Cloud use case.

## Regulatory Framework

| Reform | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Single-stage taxation (monofasia) — Diesel & Gasoline | Simplified tax, reduced arbitrage |
| Single-stage PIS/COFINS — Ethanol | Level playing field |
| Single-stage ICMS — Ethanol | Cross-state consistency |
| Naphtha Rule | Closes loophole avoiding fuel import taxes |
| CBIO enforcement | Disclosure of debtor companies + sales bans |
| Revocation of special tax regimes | Shutdown of irregular companies |

**Net Effect:** Stronger regulation and tax fairness → Margin recovery for incumbents. Formal players benefit from clearer rules.

## Refined Oil Value Chain

Even though Brazil is a major oil producer and exporter, it lacks refining capacity. Both private players and Petrobras import refined products.

- **Refined in Brazil (2024):** 75 billion liters
- **Imported (2024):** 15 billion liters
- **Domestic Refining (5-year avg):** 84%
- **Import CAGR (2017-2024):** 8.6%
- Large dependence on Petrobras (73% in 2024, down from 85% in 2017)
- Two privatized refineries: Mataripe and REAM
- Growing demand for primary infrastructure
- Increased competition from private refiners and importers
- Supply optionality increasing for distributors
- No material expansions in refining capacity expected — import dependence will continue growing

## Import Dependence

- Import CAGR: 8.6% (2017-2024)
- Total Imported: +84 billion liters (cumulative)
- Highest concentration of imports in the Northeast region
- Import sources shifting from North America to Russia
- Private players increasingly importing directly
- Import share of consumption growing year over year

## The Five Value Engines

The distributor sits at the center of the downstream value chain, orchestrating five interconnected business capabilities:

1. **Supply (Abastecimento):** Sourcing from Petrobras, imports, and managing complex contracts to ensure availability and cost competitiveness
2. **Pricing (Precificação):** Optimizing margins, managing PPI parity, and executing FX hedging strategies in a volatile market
3. **Logistics (Logística):** Operating bases, pipelines, fleets, and mastering the critical last-mile delivery to thousands of stations
4. **Commercial Execution:** Managing retail networks, large B2B accounts, and driving volume through disciplined sales operations
5. **Customer Operations:** Providing credit, loyalty programs, fleet management, and TRR services to differentiate beyond the commodity
